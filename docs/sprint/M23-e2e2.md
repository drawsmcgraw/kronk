## Findings

### 1. A listed word with `'s` is not seen: "play whatever's good" is refused (blocker)

`taste._said_words` keeps an apostrophe suffix on the word
(`[a-z0-9]+(?:'[a-z]+)?`), and `_said_generic` looks the whole token up in
`SAID_MUSIC_WORDS`. "whatever's" is not "whatever", so the sentence holds
no word for music and `/music` refuses. Only "music" is saved, by its
prefix rule ("music's"). The same holds for "something's", "anything's",
"everything's", and for the curly apostrophe.

```
$ tests/.venv/bin/python -c "from tool_service import taste; print(taste.said_request(\"play whatever's good\"))"
(False, "whatever's good")
```

Offline, both trees, the builder's fakes, the arguments the model sent:

| Request | `ff8a7a5` | HEAD |
|---|---|---|
| `{"query": "good music", "kind": "genre", "player": "Kitchen", "said": "play whatever's good in the Kitchen"}` | 200, the no-phrase pick, one pick recorded | 422 "I didn't catch what to play.", nothing sent, no pick |
| the same with no `said` | 200, the no-phrase pick | the same pick |

With the model in the loop (dev orchestrator `/api/chat`, dry run, nothing
playing; arguments from dev's shadow log):

| Sentence | `live_request` | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| play whatever's good in the Kitchen | `{"query": "good music", "kind": "genre", "player": "Kitchen"}` | "I didn't catch what to play. The closest I found was Whatever's Good For You by Sarah Critelli." |

The model sent `good music`, which cleans to no phrase, so the guard read
the sentence and refused. Before M23 the same arguments picked from the
profile.

Why it is a blocker: a reasonable person calls it a plain request for music
in general; it holds "whatever", a word on the rule's own list; it is
refused where `ff8a7a5` picked. Also refused: "play whatever's popular",
"play whatever's next".

Size of the fix: look up the word without its `'s` (list words only; "it's"
must stay out of the pronoun rule, as "play it's my life" needs). Cost: a
stripped "play Something's Gotta Give" picks, as before M23.

No test covers it: none of the 342 fixture sentences holds a listed word
with an apostrophe suffix other than "music's".

### 4. A start stamped in the pick's own second closes the window at once (should-fix, not seen in the data)

`_window_end` counts as a start any user-initiated row last played at or
after `int(pick.ts)`. Two facts make the edge thinner than it reads:

- MA stamps a row at the end of its play, not the start. In the scratch
  copy of `media_progress`, the first user-started track after pick 19 is
  stamped +418 s with 387 s played: it started near +31 s. A track cut
  short is stamped when it stops (a Cracker track at +13 s, 11 s played).
- `fully_played_only=False` lets a cut-short row count as a start.

So a track the household asked for by name, playing when "play some music"
replaces it, is stamped at the moment the pick stops it. If that stamp
falls in or after the pick's recorded second, the window ends at +0 and
every track of the pick counts as the household's. On the fake: a start at
T+0 ends the window at T+0; at T−1 it does not.

On the real data MA marked the pick 0.4 s (pick 19) to 18.9 s (pick 18)
before the pick was recorded. No row is stamped in a pick's own second:
the only user-started rows within 60 s of any of the ten picks are the
2026-11-08 bench's station plays around picks 13 to 17, the nearest 6.2 s
after one, and those were real starts. I could not produce the race
without a real play. A margin (starts count from a few seconds after the
pick) would close it.

The feature doc's "ends at the first play started on the queue" is, in
MA's records, "ends when the first item the household started finishes its
first play, or is cut short". On one queue nothing of Kronk's is stamped in
between, so the result is the same.

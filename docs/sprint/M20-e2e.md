## 2. The numbers, recomputed

Method: my own script over `~/kronk-tts-trial/runs/*.json` and
`idle/*.jsonl`, nearest-rank percentiles, compared with the tables in
`docs/bench/tts_bench_2026-11-18.md`, `M20-build.md` section 6 and the
committed `.json`.

- Speed, all sentences and by kind (30 p50/p95 pairs of first audio, plus
  total and RTF): all reproduce to the digit. Counts are 100 rows, 100
  unique (id, rep), 0 failures per engine. The committed JSON's summaries are
  byte-identical to the raw files' summaries (8 of 8).
- Cold start table: reproduces (5 of 5 rows).
- Slowdown table: reproduces (85.88 → 60.24, 85.33 → 81.92, 85.36 → 87.06,
  85.30 → 63.72, 85.34 → 68.04; TTS requests 554, 32, 33, 9, 11).
- Idle table: reproduces. Piper-only 120 samples over 19.8 min, all 0 % and
  600 MHz, 9.19 W mean. Pocket A 360 samples over 59.8 min, all 0 % and
  600 MHz, 9.35 W, zero model-server engine time. Chatterbox: the first 32
  samples (155 s) are 100 % / 2900 MHz, 37.30 W mean, p95 39.06, zero engine
  time, then 1 % / 620 MHz five seconds after the guard's SIGTERM. Turbo: 29
  samples (140 s) at 100 % / 2900 MHz, 37.50 W, then idle in five seconds.
  No request was in flight in either window (the servers' logs end with the
  cold-start requests).
- Stand-in table: reproduces (flag counts 28, 24, 23, 30, 27; length flags
  1 and 2 on `n02`; "beyond Piper" 9, 8, 13, 15, and the itemised lists add up
  to those). The rule behind "beyond Piper" is "more word errors than Piper's
  worst rep of that sentence"; against Piper's same rep it is 11, 9, 17, 16.
- Sentence-chunk figures: reproduce (Piper 0.546 / 0.572, Pocket A 2.296 /
  2.984, Pocket C 0.064 / 0.575 on three-sentence answers).
- Does not reproduce: Chatterbox service memory (finding 6).

What the questions in the brief come to:

- First audio is the same thing for every server: the first `audio-chunk`
  with a payload, timed from before the connection opens. For a server that
  sends audio only when done (Pocket A, and Chatterbox for a one-sentence
  reply) it equals total time (ratio 0.998 and 0.996), which the write-up
  says. It is first bytes, not first sound: finding 1.
- Warm-ups: Piper and Pocket had none dropped; their first-ever requests are
  the cold-start measurement, taken separately. Chatterbox and Turbo had one
  "Warming up." request per session dropped (7 and 5), as stated. No failure
  was dropped: there were none in the rows, and the lost first Chatterbox
  attempt (13 rows plus a warm-up, all successful per the driver log) is
  disclosed.
- The session method punishes Chatterbox slightly and does not flatter
  either. Chatterbox RTF p50 by rep: 2.56, 1.91, 2.42, 1.77, 1.80; the first
  counted request of a session runs at RTF 1.8 to 3.3 against 1.96 for the
  rest. A long-lived process would probably sit near RTF 1.8, about 10 %
  better than the table. Turbo shows no such drift (1.34, 1.38, 1.27, 1.22,
  1.24). The limits are missed by factors of 6 to 80, so nothing changes.
- "Alone" against "with": fair. Measuring "alone" with a GPU candidate
  unloaded puts the cost of its idle spin into "with", where it belongs;
  measuring it loaded would have flattered them. Piper loaded and idle uses
  no CPU, so its "alone" is the same thing. The comparison is unequal in
  another way: finding 4.

## 8. The recommendation

It follows. Limit by limit:

- Short confirmation within 0.5 s of Piper: Pocket C pass (right, with 0.3 s
  of margin once the leading silence is counted, not 0.48 s). Pocket A fail
  (right; in my run it fails at p50 too, +0.53 s). Chatterbox and Turbo fail.
- Three-sentence answer in 2 s: Pocket C pass, Chatterbox and Turbo fail,
  Pocket A fail by 0.15 s on one sentence (finding 5).
- Model slowdown within 10 %: Pocket A and C pass, Chatterbox and Turbo
  fail. Reading the limit as written and showing Piper's figure beside it is
  the right way round; see finding 4 for what that figure means.
- Garbled or extra audio: "waits for the listening test" is right for all
  four. The stand-in already points at one probable miss for Pocket: the
  money sentence came out as "$20,84,000" at under half the expected length
  in 4 of the builder's 10 Pocket reps (0 of my 4). If the household hears
  that, the limit as written fails, and the remedy is spelling amounts out
  before the text reaches the engine, not a different engine. The write-up
  says numbers are the thing to listen for; it could say this much.
- Idle GPU: Pocket A pass (an hour), Pocket C pass (argued, now 13 minutes
  measured), Chatterbox and Turbo fail. Not leaving a spinning ROCm process
  for an hour was correct.
- No crash or hang: pass for all four is fair. The stray children after
  SIGTERM and the kernel lines (finding 8) belong with the idle-spin note.

No limit was judged wrongly. No-go for Chatterbox and Turbo on four limits
